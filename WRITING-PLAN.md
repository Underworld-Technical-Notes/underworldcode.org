# The writing plan for Underworld Technical Notes

## What these are for

Each note explains one idea to users, in a slightly informal style, with a
worked example where the idea has one. When two or three related notes are
complete they become the basis of a modelling paper that goes through full peer
review.

**The notes are micro-preprints.** That is not a metaphor about tone -- it is
what they are. Each carries a DOI, an archived PDF, an author list with ORCIDs
and a licence, so it can be cited before the paper exists and stands on its own
if the paper never does. The paper is then assembled from work that has already
been written down, read, and criticised, rather than started from a blank page.

Three consequences worth stating, because they change how a note is written:

- **A note is a unit of explanation, not a unit of work.** Split by what a
  reader needs to understand in one sitting, not by what happened to land in
  one branch.
- **A note that will feed a paper should carry its own evidence.** A figure
  that will appear in the paper should be generated by a script in the note's
  `examples/`, not screenshotted from a run nobody can repeat.
- **Notes are versioned.** Attaching examples later, or correcting a note once
  the paper's review has sharpened an argument, means a new version of the
  deposited record. The concept DOI keeps resolving to the newest, so nothing
  already cited breaks. See `PUBLISHING.md`.

## Status key

- **published** — live, with a DOI
- **ready** — written, needs review
- **draft** — outline or raw notes exist
- **not started** — the capability may exist; the writing does not

## Next: the mesh-movers paper

The immediate priority, in order. These three are close enough in subject that
they are the first candidate paper.

### M1. MMPDE as an optimal-transport style mover — **write this next**

Status: not started.

The mover redistributes nodes to follow a metric **while keeping the mesh
topology and the parallel decomposition intact**. That is the whole point and it
is what distinguishes it from remeshing: multigrid levels survive, the
decomposition survives, and no variable transfer is needed because no node is
created or destroyed. Present it as an optimal-transport style problem — nodes
move to equalise a monitor function — rather than as a smoothing heuristic.

### M2. Stacking resolution on top

Status: not started. Follows M1 directly.

MMPDE has a fixed node budget, and moving nodes in from elsewhere is hard when
there are ridges in the metric between here and there. Stack-on-top answers
both: local, ephemeral resolution added where it is needed, keeping every MMPDE
advantage underneath. The argument for the reader is the limitation first, then
the remedy — the limitation is what makes the remedy interesting.

### M3. Stack-on-top for faults

Status: not started. **Deliberately separate from M2.**

This one is about resolving a fault, and it may belong to the meshing paper or
to the fault paper depending on how each turns out. Keeping it as its own note
means it can serve either without being rewritten.

## Representing faults in Underworld

Ripe: the capability exists in every case, and the comparison between the
approaches is the contribution. Four ways to represent a fault, and the honest
account of what each is good for is worth more than any one of them.

### F1. Weak zones

Status: not started. Almost certainly includes **transverse isotropy** — a weak
zone with a direction is a different object from a weak zone without one, and
that distinction is most of the physics.

### F2. Shear-banding plasticity

Status: not started. The band emerges rather than being placed. Localisation,
what sets the width, and what the mesh has to do with it.

### F3. Embedded weak / thin volumes

Status: not started. A fault as a resolved volume of finite thickness.

### F4. Embedded slipping surfaces

Status: not started. Zero-thickness contact; split nodes.

A fifth note comparing the four — same problem, four representations, what each
costs and what each can and cannot express — is probably the one that makes the
paper. Write it last.

## The original plan

Kept for its numbering and because several items are still exactly right.
Statuses corrected. "Not started" here means the writing has not started; in
several cases the capability landed a year ago.

### The release anchor

1. **Underworld3 Reaches 3.0.0** — draft (to be written last)
   Indexes all the others. What changed since 0.99/JOSS: new features, fixed
   bugs, API changes. Six of the seven notes the original writing order put
   ahead of this are now published, so this is close to ready to start.

### Symbolic machinery (→ Paper 1)

4. **Automatic Jacobians for Free** — not started
   Symbolic differentiation eliminating hand-coded Newton derivatives. Why this
   was impossible in UW2 (opaque C Functions). SNES integration.

6. **Constants That Aren't Constant** — not started
   The PetscDS constants mechanism routing UWexpressions to C variables. Why it
   matters for time-dependent coefficients (BDF/AM ramp, continuation).

8. **Natural Mathematical Syntax in Scientific Python** — not started
   The MathematicalMixin: why `density * velocity` works, and how operator
   overloading connects to SymPy's Matrix API.

### Units and scaling (→ Paper 1)

10. **Non-dimensionalisation Without Tears** — not started
    Reference quantities, nondimensional unwrapping, the solver working in
    scaled space while the user thinks in physical units.

### Particles and surfaces (→ Paper 2)

13. **Stress Has a History** — not started. See the VEP cluster above; this may
    be where that cluster starts.

14. **Ghost Boundaries Done Right** — not started
    Internal and external boundary integrals in parallel: the ownership
    problem, PETSc patches, MPI-correct integration. The boundary-flux and
    rotated-BC work since makes this much more concrete than when it was listed.

15. **Tracking Interfaces in Large Deformation** — not started
    Material interfaces, level-set weighted composites, population control.

### Geometry and meshing (→ Paper 2)

16. **Meshing for Planetary Scale** — not started
    Cubed-sphere construction, RegionalSphericalBox, geodetic projections. Why
    lat-lon grids have singularities.

17. **Geographic Meshes for Regional Geodynamics** — not started
    Ellipsoidal Earth geometry with topography, RegionalGeographicBox, boundary
    labelling, coordinate transforms.

18. **Symbolic Geometry: Differential Operators in Curvilinear Coordinates** — not started
    How gradient/divergence/curl auto-adjust via the coordinate system factory.

19. **Adaptive Meshes That Follow the Physics** — superseded by M1–M3, which
    say the same thing with a year's more work behind them. Kept so the number
    still resolves.

### Infrastructure and craft

21. **Safe Parallelism Without MPI Expertise** — not started
    `uw.pprint()`, `selective_ranks()`, why PETSc handles the hard parts.

22. **From Notebook to Supercomputer in Zero Edits** — not started
    The same notebook on a laptop and on 12,000 cores.

23. **Interactive 3D Geodynamics in a Browser** — not started
    PyVista/trame integration, P2 field visualisation, server proxy detection.

25. **Notation Gymnastics in Continuum Mechanics** — not started
    Voigt, Mandel, full tensor. Why Mandel preserves inner products.

## Other candidates from the last year

Not in the original plan, because the work did not exist when it was written.
Listed as candidates, not commitments.

- **Multigrid on meshes that were cut.** Why a cut is not a multigrid level,
  and what the coarse levels have to carry.
- **Rotated free-slip, and getting the normal traction out.** Machine-precision
  wall-normal flow on curved boundaries, and the reaction as dynamic topography.
- **The analytic solution suite.** `uw.analytic`, and testing a solver against
  something that cannot be argued with.
- **Nonlinear solvers that start well.** Warm starts, yield homotopy, and what
  a converged reason does not tell you.
