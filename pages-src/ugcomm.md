The Underworld geodynamics group works on scientific problems, computational
algorithms, and software for Earth and planetary dynamics. Everything we produce
is released under open source licences, so that anyone can reproduce our results
and build on them.

Our particular interest is the long-term evolution of the Earth, and helping
other people run time-evolving models of plate- and lithosphere-scale problems.

The group is based in Australia — at the Australian National University, the
University of Melbourne and Monash University — with collaborators across the
(under)world.

## Governance

Underworld is community research infrastructure, funded by
[AuScope](https://www.auscope.org.au/) as part of the Australian Government's
National Collaborative Research Infrastructure Strategy (NCRIS).

Development is led from the Research School of Earth Sciences at the Australian
National University and overseen by a steering committee drawn from the
supporting organisations and from the user community. Day-to-day decisions —
what gets built, what gets fixed, what gets released — happen in the open, on
the [issue tracker](https://github.com/underworldcode/underworld3/issues) and in
pull requests, and that record is the authoritative one.

Additional support has come from the Australian Research Council, the
[NeCTAR](https://www.nectar.org.au/) eResearch Tools programme, and the
[National Computational Infrastructure](https://nci.org.au/). Underworld 2 was
originally developed with the Victorian Partnership for Advanced Computing.

Public routes come first on purpose. A question asked in the issue tracker is
answered where the next person with the same question will find it; a question
asked by email is answered once.

## The wider ecosystem

We also develop other packages, which have homes of their own:
[stripy](https://github.com/underworldcode/stripy) for triangulation in the
plane and on the sphere, and
[quagmire](https://github.com/underworldcode/quagmire) for surface processes.

Models built with Underworld and shared by the people who built them are
collected at the [model exchange](/underworld-model-exchange/).
